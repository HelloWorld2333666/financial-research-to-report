**Purpose:** a deck that reads as one argument, with one identity and varied page structures where the content calls for them.

**Storyboard first.** For each slide, record:
- **role:** navigation, statement, dense evidence, focused evidence, synthesis;
- **objective and message;**
- **candidate families;**
- **pattern;**
- **density;**
- **focal figure;**
- **visual assets:** imagery (cover, dividers, closing), map, flags, logos, icons. A deck about places, companies or products that uses none of them needs a reason.

The action titles in sequence should read as the executive summary (H1).

**Page roles and typical patterns:**

| Role | Patterns |
|---|---|
| Image-led cover or divider | P7, P5 |
| Statement | P6, A1 |
| Multi-panel dashboard | P3 |
| Dominant chart | P1, or a chart with commentary beneath |
| Chart–table combination | P2, P4 |
| Geographical analysis | P6 with V10; P3 zoom chain |
| Relationship diagram | V11–V13, with or without a panel |
| Comparison matrix | V09 scorecard page |
| Executive conclusion | H1 numbered synthesis; P5 |

**Consistency comes from identity, not layout:**
- typography roles;
- colour roles;
- spacing;
- the header system;
- reporting conventions (C1).

**Checks after planning** (flagged, not enforced):
- **Runs of the same pattern** without a reason.
- **The share of P1 pages**: watch it, don't cap it. P1 is valuable, not the default.
- **Narrative transitions:** dividers and trackers where the argument turns.
- **Recommendations:**
  - follow dense pages with lighter ones where the narrative allows;
  - avoid repeating the same focal figure on adjacent slides unless deliberate (ST4).

**Justified repetition:** analytically parallel pages (country profiles, scenarios) may share a layout on purpose. Record why in the storyboard.
