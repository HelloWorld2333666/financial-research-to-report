**Classes:**
- **H:** hard constraint (meaning, integrity, legibility).
- **P:** recommended practice.
- **J:** judgement in context.

**Only H is enforced.**

**R1. Focal point follows the objective.** *(P; balance J)*
- Analytical slides give the main evidence the most emphasis.
- Covers, dividers and statements may let imagery, typography or a key figure lead.

**R2. Colour has meaning.** *(H within a visualization; P across the deck; J for the palette)*
- Roles:
  - sequential for magnitude;
  - diverging for signed values;
  - categorical for groups;
  - highlight for emphasis;
  - neutral for context.
- **Within each visualization** a colour keeps one meaning.
- **Across the deck** the palette stays coherent; the same colour need not mean the same entity everywhere.
- Coordinated multi-colour palettes are fine; keep them readable and accessible.

**R3. Refined separators.** *(P)*
- Subtle outlines are fine when they help grouping.
- Avoid heavy or awkward borders between coloured regions.
- Prefer fills, white gutters and spacing.

**R4. Edge-bleeding fields are a technique, not a rule.** *(J)* Floating cards, margins and internal grouping are equally valid.

**R5. Lines are intentional.** *(P; weight J)*
- Lines may be structural, analytical, navigational or decorative.
- Structural lines don't compete with the data.

**R6. Clear type hierarchy.** *(H legibility; P targets; J typeface)*
- Targets:
  - titles about 30–36 pt;
  - body about 12 pt or more where possible;
  - labels, footnotes and sources smaller as needed.
- Weight before size.

**R7. Whitespace by balance.** *(J)* Avoid accidental emptiness; keep intentional negative space.

**R8. Images integrated deliberately.** *(P; choice J)*
- Recolour or duotone, crop, cut out, layer.
- Contained image cards are also fine.

**R9. Consistent icons.** *(P; placement J)* Consistent in style, scale, alignment and quality, wherever they are placed.

**R10. Relationships clear.** *(P; connectors J)* Through colour, alignment, proximity and labels; a visible connector when it genuinely helps.

**R11. Shared scales for direct comparison.** *(H when comparing)* Different scales only for different measures, clearly labelled.

**R12. Decoration and fidelity.** *(J)* Don't enlarge crude, low-detail illustrations excessively.

**R13. Dark at any scale.** *(J)* Full slide, field, card, label or mark, judged by balance and hierarchy.

**R14. Learn without copying defects.** *(H for our output)* No broken labels, overlaps or misalignment.

## Critique procedure (after each render)

1. **H first.** Read the QA and integrity findings. Check the router's hard constraints on the image. Any failure goes back to the plan.
2. **P next.** For each region: does the focal point follow the objective (R1)? Are separators refined (R3)? Is every line intentional (R5)? Is the hierarchy clear and labels legible (R6)? Is the relationship to the explanation clear (R10)? Is a dense page stripped to essentials (R11)?
3. **J last, with a reference beside the render:**
   - the overall balance of masses;
   - whether the whitespace is intentional;
   - whether the imagery's scale suits its fidelity;
   - whether the page looks assembled or composed.
4. **Write findings** as targeted plan edits, tagged H, P or J. Revise at most twice. Report any remaining J findings to the user rather than forcing them.
