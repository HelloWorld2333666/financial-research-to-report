**Purpose:** one proof, one meaning. A dominant exhibit beside a panel that says what it means, or that holds the decisive figures.

**Applies when:** a single-claim slide with one main exhibit.

**Regions and roles:**
- **evidence:** the dominant exhibit, the larger share;
- **conclusion** (implications) **or anchors** (figures).

**Variants:**
- **Implications panel:** heading plus a few statements, with the key phrases in bold.
- **Figures panel:** two or three anchors (A1), solid or dark (T56).
- **Panel treatment:** a field bleeding to the slide edge, **or** floating cards, **or** a commentary column with no field (R4).

**Combines with:** any V family as the exhibit; D1 bridging badge or arrow at the boundary; H1 conclusion hierarchy.

**Risks:**
- **A valuable pattern, but not the default.** The deck plan watches how often it is used and how long its runs are, and allows repetition for analytically parallel slides (DK).
- **Fails when:**
  - the panel repeats the chart;
  - the panel's border is heavier than its content (R3);
  - every slide in a row uses it without reason.

**Execution:**
- The exhibit takes most of the width.
- The panel is calmer than the evidence (R1).
- The title spans the evidence region or the full width.
